Sugarcane Price in Maharashtra — FRP, Mill Rates & Selling Guide

Sugarcane is a unique crop in India, primarily sold to sugar mills rather than open mandis. Understand the Fair and Remunerative Price (FRP) and other factors affecting sugarcane prices in Maharashtra.

Understand Sugarcane Selling

For sugarcane, the selling process is different from other crops. Instead of finding the 'best mandi', farmers typically sell to sugar mills at a government-fixed price.

How Sugarcane is Sold in Maharashtra

Unlike most other crops that are sold in APMC mandis through open auctions, sugarcane in Maharashtra is primarily sold directly to sugar mills. This system is governed by specific regulations to ensure fair prices for farmers.

  • Fair and Remunerative Price (FRP): The central government announces the FRP for sugarcane annually. This is the minimum price that sugar mills are legally obligated to pay to farmers.
  • Mill Agreements: Farmers typically enter into agreements with nearby sugar mills for the supply of their cane.
  • Payment in Installments: Payments from mills are often made in installments, which can affect a farmer's cash flow.
  • Recovery Rate: The FRP is linked to the sugar recovery rate from the cane, meaning higher sugar content can sometimes lead to better effective prices.

Factors Affecting Sugarcane Price

While FRP sets a base, other factors can influence the overall returns for sugarcane farmers:

  • Sugar Recovery Rate: Higher sugar content in cane means better returns for mills, potentially leading to additional payments above FRP (State Advised Price - SAP in some states, though less common in Maharashtra).
  • Mill Efficiency and Financial Health: The ability of a mill to pay promptly and offer additional incentives can vary.
  • Transportation Distance: While not a direct price factor, longer transport distances to mills increase costs for farmers.
  • Government Policies: Export/import policies for sugar and ethanol blending mandates can indirectly affect mill profitability and farmer payments.

Sugarcane Trends in Maharashtra

Maharashtra is one of India's largest sugarcane-producing states, with a significant number of cooperative and private sugar mills. The crushing season typically runs from October/November to April/May.

Understanding the announced FRP, the performance of local sugar mills, and government policies is key for sugarcane farmers. Fasalo aims to provide relevant information to help farmers navigate these specific market dynamics.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is sugarcane typically sold in Maharashtra?

In Maharashtra, sugarcane is primarily sold directly to sugar mills, not through open auctions in APMC mandis like other crops. The government sets a Fair and Remunerative Price (FRP) that mills are legally bound to pay farmers.

What is FRP (Fair and Remunerative Price) for sugarcane?

FRP is the minimum price that sugar mills must pay to sugarcane farmers. It is fixed by the central government based on recommendations from the Commission for Agricultural Costs and Prices (CACP) and is linked to the recovery rate of sugar from cane.

Does Fasalo's 'Find Best Market' tool work for sugarcane?

Fasalo's 'Find Best Market' tool is designed for crops sold in open APMC mandis where prices fluctuate daily. For sugarcane, which is primarily sold to mills at government-fixed FRP, the tool's direct market comparison functionality may not be applicable in the same way. We recommend understanding the FRP and mill payment schedules for sugarcane.
