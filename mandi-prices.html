Mandi Prices in India: What Farmers Need to Know

Understanding mandi prices is crucial for maximizing your crop's value. Fasalo helps you navigate the complexities of agricultural markets across India.

How Mandi Prices Are Determined

Mandi (Agricultural Produce Market Committee or APMC) markets are central to agricultural trade in India. Prices are typically determined through an open auction system, influenced by several factors:

  • Supply and Demand: The most fundamental factor. High arrivals (supply) and low demand can drive prices down, and vice-versa.
  • Crop Quality: Better quality produce often fetches higher prices.
  • Seasonality: Prices can fluctuate significantly with harvest seasons. Post-harvest gluts often lead to lower prices.
  • Local Factors: Weather conditions, local festivals, and even road connectivity can impact prices in a specific mandi.
  • Transport Costs: The cost of transporting produce to a mandi directly impacts a farmer's net profit.

Why Mandi Prices Vary Across Locations

It's common for the price of the same crop to differ between mandis, even those relatively close to each other. This variation is due to localized supply-demand imbalances, transport infrastructure, and the presence of different buyers or processors.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a Mandi and how does it work?

A Mandi (Agricultural Produce Market Committee or APMC market) is a marketplace where farmers sell their produce to traders through an auction process. Prices are determined by supply and demand on a given day.

Why do mandi prices vary across different locations?

Mandi prices vary due to local supply and demand, transport costs, quality of produce, seasonal factors, and the presence of buyers. A mandi with high demand and low supply will generally have higher prices.
